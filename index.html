What am I looking at?

Every note gets measured against the root. That distance is an interval, and the interval number is what theory is actually built out of.

R is the root. 3 is a major third (4 frets up). ♭3 is a minor third (3 frets up) — that one semitone is the whole difference between a major and a minor chord. 5 is the perfect fifth, 7 frets up, the most stable sound against the root.

Switch to Scale degrees to see the same shapes numbered 1–7 inside the major scale. Those numbers are what the Numbers tab uses.

How a chord gets built

Take the major scale. Start on any degree, then skip a note, take one, skip a note, take one. Stacking those thirds is the whole trick — and the scale decides on its own whether the result is major, minor, or diminished. You are not choosing the quality. The key is.

Reading the numerals

A roman numeral answers two questions at once: which scale degree is the root of this chord, and what quality did the scale hand that degree.

Uppercase means major. Lowercase means minor. A little circle means diminished. So vi is “build a chord on the 6th degree, and it comes out minor.”

This is why numerals beat chord names for describing music: ii–V–I is the same motion in every key. Change the key and the letters change, but the numbers — and the sound — do not.
